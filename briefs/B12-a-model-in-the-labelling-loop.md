# B.12 A model in the labelling loop

## Candidate's half

Read only this section before starting the clock.

"Our labelling costs have tripled this year. The data team wants to put a model in the loop to pre-label everything. What do you think?"

---

## Interviewer's half

Give the numbers only if asked. Deliver the three pushes in the questions block whatever the candidate has said, and the twist at minute thirty. Score against the rubric notes below and the sheets in `../rubrics/`; the notes name the row that decides the brief, never the design.

### Numbers, if asked

Five hundred thousand labels a month across eight projects, at prices from cents to dollars; two of the projects are half the spend; agreement between two annotators is seventy to ninety per cent by project; the models the labels train are evaluated on held-out labels from the same annotators; the data team's proposal is that annotators correct a model's labels rather than label from scratch.

### Pushes

Annotators accept the model's label ninety-five per cent of the time; is that good news? The held-out evaluation set was pre-labelled too. Which projects should not have a model in the loop at all?

### Twist, at minute thirty

The two expensive projects are the ones whose labels go into a fairness audit.

### Rubric notes

Decided on Recovery, in the staff sense. Above the bar turns the request into the problem, which is the two projects that are half the spend, and asks what each label is for before pricing the loop. Above the bar at Close the loop names the anchoring feedback loop, annotators accepting the model, as the design's harm, keeps a from-scratch stratum and a from-scratch evaluation set as walls, and says which projects the loop must not touch, with a number. Above the bar at Choose sequences the change by reversibility, one project at a time, with agreement rate as the instrument, and refuses the pre-labelled evaluation set aloud.
