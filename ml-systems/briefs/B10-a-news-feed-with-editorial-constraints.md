# B.10 A news feed with editorial constraints

## Candidate's half

Read only this section before starting the clock.

A news product wants a personalised front page, and its editors want to keep control of what the front page is.

---

## Interviewer's half

Give the numbers only if asked. Deliver the three pushes in the questions block whatever the candidate has said, and the twist at minute thirty. Score against the rubric notes below and the sheets in `../rubrics/`; the notes name the row that decides the brief, never the design.

### Numbers, if asked

Two million readers; ten thousand articles a week; the editors pick eight stories a day that must appear for everyone; the business is subscriptions; clicks, dwell time and subscriptions are logged; readers who see only one kind of story cancel more; articles are stale within a day.

### Pushes

The model's click rate is up nine per cent in the A/B and the editors say the front page is unrecognisable. How would you measure diversity, and what would you do if it fell? An article is corrected after publication; what happens to the readers who were shown the original?

### Twist, at minute thirty

The editors ask for a report each morning of which stories the model is suppressing and why.

### Rubric notes

Decided on Objective and scored double on Close the loop. Above the bar treats the editors' eight as slots and diversity as a guardrail with a measurement, and says what clicks are a proxy for and why dwell and subscription disagree with them; sizes the A/B on subscriptions, which is the slow read. Above the bar at Close the loop names the narrowing feedback loop, the correction's rollback, and the report as the harm row made legible. Above the bar at Bound sees that freshness binds and the fleet does not.
